## Wizard

Wizard is a trick-taking card game for three to six players. The deck has four colored suits with ranks 1 to 13, plus four Wizard cards and four Jester cards. One player deals each round. After every round, the dealer role moves one seat clockwise.

The number of cards dealt rises with the round number. In the first round each player receives one card; in the second round two cards; and so on, until a final round in which the whole deck is dealt. Cards that are not dealt stay as a face-down pile in the center. After the deal, the top card of that pile is turned up. An ordinary color card sets the trump suit for the round. A Jester means there is no trump. A Wizard lets the dealer choose the trump suit after looking at their own hand. In the last round there is no pile left, so there is no trump.

After players look at their hands, they bid how many tricks they think they will win. Bidding starts with the player to the left of the dealer and continues clockwise. The same player also leads the first card of the first trick. Later tricks are led by the winner of the previous trick. Players must follow the led color if they can. Wizard and Jester cards may always be played. The first Wizard in a trick wins it. If a Wizard leads, every later card is legal and the first Wizard still wins. If a Jester leads, the next ordinary color card sets the color that must be followed, unless a Wizard appears first and keeps the trick colorless. Scoring rewards an exact bid and penalizes misses. The highest score after the last round wins.

## Abalone

Abalone is a two-player marble game on a hexagonal board. Each side starts with exactly 14 marbles in the fixed starting layout shown in the rulebook. On a turn, a player moves one, two, or three of their own marbles one step in the same direction. Multi-marble moves must form one straight contiguous line.

A player may push opposing marbles only in a straight inline Sumito, and only with strict numerical superiority of the pushing group. Broadside pushes are not allowed, and equal groups cannot push each other. A marble pushed off the board is removed. The first player to push six opposing marbles off the board wins.

## Exploding Kittens

Exploding Kittens is a last-player-standing card game. Each player starts with a hand that already includes one Defuse card. On a normal turn, a player may play action cards and then must end the turn by drawing the top card of the draw pile. The danger in the pile is the Exploding Kitten. If a player draws it and has no Defuse left, that player is eliminated. The last remaining player wins.

Defuse lets a player survive a drawn Exploding Kitten and secretly put it back somewhere in the draw pile. Other cards change risk and turn flow. Skip ends the current turn without drawing. Attack ends the attacker's turn without drawing and forces the next living player to take two turns in a row. If that player also plays Attack, the following player then owes exactly two turns. Favor forces a chosen living opponent to give one card of their choice. Two cards of the same title can be played as a pair to steal one random card from a chosen opponent. For both Favor and pair effects, an opponent with an empty hand is not a useful target, because no card can be taken. When a player is eliminated, that player's remaining hand and the Exploding Kitten go to the discard pile.

## Bohnanza

Bohnanza is a planting and trading card game. Bean cards are planted in fields, and each field may hold only one bean type. Harvesting a field pays coins according to the printed beanometer for that bean type.

A turn has four phases. First, the active player must plant the front card of their hand into a field; planting a second hand card is optional, and a third is not allowed. Second, the active player reveals cards and may trade with the others. Unequal trades are allowed, but only the active player may trade, and received cards do not enter the hand. Third, every player must plant all cards received in the trade or reveal step and may choose the planting order of those cards. Fourth, the active player draws new cards. Players may also harvest fields during the game, including outside their own turn, following the harvest limits in the rulebook. At the end of the game, remaining fields are harvested for coins, while cards still in hand do not score.

## CATAN

CATAN is a resource and building board game. In the version used here, four players play on the fixed beginner board. A turn follows a strict order: the active player must roll the dice, may then trade, and may then build. Resources come from settlements and cities next to numbered terrain hexes. Players spend resources to build roads, settlements, and cities, or to buy development cards.

Development cards include Knights and progress cards such as Road Building, Year of Plenty, and Monopoly. An eligible development card may be played during the active turn, including before the dice roll, but a card bought on the same turn cannot be played yet, except for an immediate win with a victory-point card. Special awards include Longest Road and Largest Army. Longest Road needs a continuous road of at least five segments; branches are not added together, a longer road can take the card, and an opposing settlement or city can break a route. Domestic trade is only with the active player, and maritime trade uses harbors. Further detail for many terms is given in the matching Almanac, which the base rulebook points to.

## 6 nimmt! (optional)

Include only if this game appears later in the thesis.

6 nimmt! is a simultaneous card-placement game with four open rows. Each turn, every player secretly chooses one hand card. After all choices are made, the cards are revealed together and placed in rising number order. A card normally goes into the row whose end is just below it and closest in value. If a card is lower than every row end, its owner must choose one row and take all cards from that row as penalty points. A sixth card in a row also forces its owner to take the five cards already there. Penalty points come from the cattle heads printed on the cards. The game continues until a player has collected more than 66 cattle heads; the player with the fewest cattle heads wins.
